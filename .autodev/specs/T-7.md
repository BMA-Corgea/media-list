# T-7 — The queue: one global order, filterable by kind

**Type** feature · **Risk** medium · **Intent** T-1 · **After** T-4

Evan's explicit choice: **one global queue**, not a queue per genre. Filters narrow the
*view*; they never fork the order. Includes `game` per the amendment.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **AC1 — one ordered list.** A queue view showing every queued title in `queue_position`
   order, with its poster, kind, year and `why`.
2. **AC2 — drag to reorder.** Grab a row, drop it elsewhere, and the new order **persists
   across a reload**.
3. **AC3 — the carousel agrees.** Reordering here changes the order on the wall (T-5), since
   both read the same single sequence.
4. **AC4 — filter chips.** `all · anime · movie · live-action · game` narrow the view. Chip
   state is visible. Counts reflect the filter.
5. **AC5 — THE HARD ONE: reordering inside a filter must not corrupt the global order.**
   Filter to games, move one game above another, clear the filter: the two games have swapped
   **and every non-game title has kept its position relative to everything else**. This is
   the criterion that decides whether the "one global queue" promise is real.
6. **AC6 — gap exhaustion is handled.** Positions are gap-tolerant (T-4 appends at max+10,
   T-6 prepends at min−10). When two neighbours end up adjacent with no integer between them,
   the queue renumbers and completes the move rather than silently refusing it. Proven by
   forcing the collision.
7. **AC7 — keyboard reordering.** A focused row moves with `Alt+↑` / `Alt+↓`. Drag-only
   reordering is unusable without a mouse.
8. **AC8 — one title, and zero titles, do not break it.**
9. **AC9 — skin-driven and reduced-motion safe**, like every surface: no colour literals,
   tokens only.

## Out of scope

The wheel (T-8), reviews and the Seen archive (T-9), CSV (T-10). Seen titles are not shown
here — this view is the *queue*.
